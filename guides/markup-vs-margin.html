Markup vs profit margin: why 20% isn't 20%

Markup and margin both describe profit as a percentage, but they measure it against different numbers. Mixing them up is one of the easiest ways to underprice a job without noticing.

The short version

  • Markup is profit as a percentage of your cost.
  • Margin is profit as a percentage of the selling price.

The price is always bigger than the cost, so the same profit is always a smaller percentage as a margin than as a markup.

The formulas

Markup % = Profit ÷ Cost × 100

Margin % = Profit ÷ Price × 100

To get a price from a margin you want:

Price = Cost ÷ (1 − Margin)

For example, for a 25% margin: Price = Cost ÷ 0.75.

Worked example

Your total cost for a job is £1,000.

MethodPriceProfitReal margin
Add a 20% markup£1,200£20016.7%
Price for a 20% margin£1,250£25020%

The difference is £50 on a £1,000 job. Across a year of jobs it adds up fast.

To convert yourself: Markup = Margin ÷ (1 − Margin), and Margin = Markup ÷ (1 + Markup), using decimals (20% = 0.2).

Why a margin can never reach 100%

A 100% margin would mean the whole price is profit and the job cost nothing. That's why the calculator caps margin at 90%. Markup has no such limit: a 100% markup simply doubles your cost.

Which one should you use? Either works, as long as you know which one you're using. Margin is handy because it tells you directly how much of every pound you charge you keep.
