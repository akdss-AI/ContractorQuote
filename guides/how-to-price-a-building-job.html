How to price a building job, step by step

A good price covers everything the job costs you, pays its share of running the business, and leaves a profit. Here's how to build one up, in the same order as the ContractorQuote calculator.

1. Labour

Estimate the total hours for everyone on the job and multiply by the hourly rate. If it's just you, use the rate you need to pay yourself, not what you'd like to charge.

Labour = Hours × Hourly rate

Be honest about hours. Include setting up, clearing away, waiting for deliveries and snagging.

2. Labour burden

An hour of labour costs more than the hourly rate. The extra is the "burden": things like time off, pension contributions, insurance, protective gear, travel time and the admin hours nobody pays you for. It's entered as a percentage of labour.

Labour burden = Labour × Burden %

The right figure depends on your own situation. If you're not sure, add up a year of these costs and divide by a year of labour cost to get your own percentage. The true cost of labour guide walks through this with a worked example.

3. Materials

Total the materials for the job at the price you'll actually pay, including delivery. Allow for waste and cuts.

4. Subcontractors

Add what you'll pay other trades for their part of the job, such as an electrician, plumber or plasterer. Get their prices in writing where you can.

5. Other job costs

Costs that only exist because of this job: skip hire, scaffold, plant and tool hire, parking, waste disposal, specialist fixings.

6. Overhead

Overhead is what it costs to run the business whether or not you're on a job: the van, fuel, phone, insurance, tools, software and accountant. Each job needs to cover a fair share. ContractorQuote adds overhead as a percentage of the job's direct costs.

Overhead = (Labour + Burden + Materials + Subcontractors + Other) × Overhead %

A simple way to find your own percentage: divide a year's overhead by a year's direct job costs.

7. Profit margin and price

Add everything up to get the total job cost. Then choose the share of the selling price you want to keep as profit (your margin) and work out the price:

Price = Total job cost ÷ (1 − Margin)

Profit isn't greed. It pays for quiet weeks, replacing kit, jobs that overrun and growing the business. See markup vs margin for why adding 20% to cost doesn't give a 20% margin.

8. Write a clear quote

  • Scope: say exactly what's included.
  • Exclusions: say what isn't, so there are no arguments later.
  • Payment terms: when and how you expect to be paid.
  • Validity: how long the price stands, because material prices change.

Your customer sees the work and the price. They don't need to see your costs, overhead or profit, and ContractorQuote never prints them. For a printable layout and a filled-in example, see the free builder's quote template.
