Free builder's quote template (UK)

A clear, written quote tells your customer exactly what they're paying for and stops arguments later. This page gives you a blank template you can print or save as a PDF, a filled-in example, and a checklist of what to include. It's free, with no sign-up.

Two ways to use it

  1. Fill it in by hand: print the blank template below and write in the details.
  2. Let the calculator fill it in: enter your costs and profit margin in the ContractorQuote calculator. It works out the price, adds your details, and prints a finished quote in the same layout. Your costs and profit are never shown on it.

What to include in a building quote

  • Your details: business or trading name, your name, address, phone and email.
  • The customer: their name and the address where the work will happen.
  • Quote number and date: so you can both refer to it later.
  • Scope of work: exactly what you will do, in plain language. One item per line works well.
  • Exclusions: what isn't included, such as decorating, skip permits or unforeseen structural work.
  • The price: a total, with a simple breakdown if it helps your customer understand it.
  • Payment terms: when and how you expect to be paid, including any deposit or stage payments you've agreed.
  • How long the price is valid: material prices change, so give a date the quote runs until.
  • Acceptance: space for the customer to sign and date, or a note on how to accept.

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice. If you have your own terms and conditions, attach them or refer to them in the notes.

Common quoting mistakes

  • A vague scope. "Build wall" leaves room for disagreement. Say what, where, how big and in what material.
  • No exclusions. If something isn't included, write it down.
  • No end date. Without a "valid until" date, a customer may expect the same price months later.
  • Forgetting the hidden costs. Labour costs more than the hourly rate, and the van, insurance and tools have to be paid for too. The pricing guide explains labour burden and overhead.
  • Mixing up markup and margin. Adding 20% to your costs gives a 16.7% margin, not 20%. See markup vs margin.
